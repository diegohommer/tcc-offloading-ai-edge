\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}
%==============================================================================

As the use of Large Language Models (LLMs) becomes widespread, both at work and at
home~\cite{bick2026rapid}, the challenge of adapting the network architecture to handle 
this rapidly evolving technology remains an active research
topic~\cite{chen2026network}. Since evolution seldom happens in isolation, the network
itself has also been undergoing changes, one of which is the adoption of Passive Optical
Network (PON) technology in the access network~\cite{effenberger2007introduction}. Driven
by the demand for greater capacity, lower latency, and reduced operating costs, PON is
being deployed alongside or in place of the hybrid fiber-coaxial (HFC)
plant~\cite{prodnik2025migration}. Quality of service, scalability, security, and energy
cost are some of the broad topics that need to be taken into account as both of these
changes happen in parallel.


On the models' side, cost is a concern as well, since LLM inference is
expensive~\cite{chen2026network}. One way of making it cheaper is the model cascade: a
smaller model answers what it can, and the query is only passed to a larger one when
its answer is not reliable enough~\cite{chen2024frugalgpt}. What counts as expensive
depends on the setting, from the price of an API call to the energy spent answering
each query, and energy is the cost this work is concerned with. Treating it as a cost
of its own is the idea behind Green AI, which argues that efficiency should be reported alongside
accuracy~\cite{schwartz2020green}, and some serving systems already use it to decide
which model answers a query: GreenServ picks a model from a heterogeneous pool based on
the accuracy and energy it has observed, and uses 31\% less energy than routing at
random~\cite{ziller2026greenserv}.

These systems choose which model answers, but not where it runs, which is a separate
question. With edge computing, inference is no longer confined to the cloud, but spread
across end devices, edge nodes, and cloud servers, each tier further from the user than
the one below, and with more computing power~\cite{chen2026network}. RecServe puts the two ideas together, placing a cascade on
the tiers of such a network and letting each tier decide, from the confidence scores it
has seen recently, whether to answer the query or to pass it up~\cite{wu2025recserve}.
Following RecServe's decision rule, \citeonline{pakpahan2026continuum} bring tiered
LLM inference into the access network itself, equipping Optical Network Units (ONUs)
and Optical Line Terminals (OLTs) to run part of the inference, so that a Time and
Wavelength Division Multiplexed PON (TWDM-PON) provides the edge and fog tiers of
such a hierarchy. In both works, however, a tier decides whether to pass a query up
from confidence alone, and the energy spent at each tier plays no part in that
decision.

In this work, we propose an energy-aware version of RecServe, in which a tier decides
whether to answer a query or pass it up based not only on its confidence, but also on
the energy spent answering at each tier. As a case study, we simulate a three-tier
hierarchy over a PON, formed by the user device, the ONU and the OLT, each running a
larger model than the one below. We evaluate two energy-aware strategies: one follows
a fixed timetable of the OLT's cost, while the other takes advantage of the PON
downstream broadcast, with the OLT periodically announcing its current cost to every
ONU. Both are compared with RecServe at the same target accuracy, in energy per query
and in communication burden, under recorded conversation traffic with and without
unforeseen surges. \todo{one or two sentences with the main results, once the Results
chapter is settled.}

The contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item An energy-aware version of RecServe, in which the decision to answer a query
  or pass it up takes into account the energy spent at each tier, and not only
  confidence;
  \item An energy model of the user device, the ONU and the OLT under a single
  measurement boundary, with the OLT's energy measured by us on a GPU;
  \item An offloading strategy that uses the PON downstream broadcast to share the
  OLT's current cost with every ONU;
  \item A simulation study on recorded conversation traffic, with and without
  unforeseen surges, comparing the energy-aware strategies with RecServe in energy per
  query and communication burden.
\end{itemize}
